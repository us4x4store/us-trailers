## 1. Buyer profiles

Evidence sources: competitor positioning (sections 2 and 5), ZA autocomplete data (section 4), and one public dispute case. Budgets are market price bands from section 5, not buyer surveys.

### 1.1 The overlander (4x4 couple or family, 40–65, Land Cruiser/Hilux/Ranger/Prado owner)
- **Wants:** a trailer that survives Namibia, Botswana and the Kgalagadi corrugations. Quick set-up. Real off-grid power (lithium, Victron, solar). Water. Kitchen. A permanent bed. Often a specific layout no catalogue model offers. Competitors sell on this: Invader Viator spec sheet lists Victron, LiFePO4 and a 150 L tank (https://invader.co.za/invader-viator/). BERG sells "Over R100K included" (https://www.bergoffroad.com/).
- **Fears:** dust and water ingress. Chassis cracks. Leaf springs or welds failing far from help. Weight (GVM over the vehicle's rating, EB licence, the 100 km/h bakkie limit). Poor after-sales. Resale value. Paperwork failures. Public case: a Somerset West buyer is suing over a R1.25 m Ng'Ombe off-road caravan after 14 defects in two weeks, including an **unregistered vehicle** (Weekend Argus via https://openvan.camp/en/news/incident/legal-dispute-over-luxury-offroad-caravan).
- **Trusts:** proof of real trips (Kaokoland photos, owner stories). Named components (Knott, Burquip, Victron, Al-Ko). Galvanised chassis. A visible workshop and founder. NRCS homologation (Metalian makes this claim). Demo units and rentals (Conqueror, Kampi hire). Awards. Owner communities.
- **Budget:** new compact off-road campers start at about **R185k–R420k** (Tonga R184k, Conqueror Comfort Platinum from about R291k, Bushwakka from R314k excl VAT). Off-road caravans run **R450k–R900k+** (Air Opus R455k, Gecko R555k, BERG from R649 950, Conqueror UEV-25 used R690k–R850k). See section 5.
- **Search terms:** "off road trailers for sale south africa", "4x4 camping trailers for sale western cape", "off road caravans south africa prices", "teardrop trailer south africa".

### 1.2 The "toys" owner (motocross/enduro family, adventure bikers, quad/side-by-side, jet ski or boat owners)
- **Wants:** to carry 2–3 bikes or a quad/SxS **and** sleep or work out of the same trailer: a toy hauler. Secure, dust-proof transport. A ramp. Tie-downs. A fuel and water bowser. Tool storage. Washing gear. A gazebo/awning for race weekends.
- **Fears:** theft at events. Stone damage on gravel (Wild Dog commission: "lockable hinged canopy that protects the bikes from stone damage", https://wilddog.net.za/posts/5196159/). Ramp angle too steep. Licence code and weight. A "toy hauler" in SA usually means an imported US RV that is too big and heavy: an RV Fuzion 40F listed at **R950 000** in Johannesburg (https://expeditionmeister.com/trailers-caravans-off-road/2-axles-trailer/rv-fuzion-40f-toy-hauler-new-i173).
- **Trusts:** builders who ride. Clever details (bike chocks, fuel separation). Video of loading and unloading.
- **Budget:** plain 3-bike trailers sell for **R40k–R130k** (used/locally built: Gumtree R80k and R130k listings). An enclosed toy hauler with sleeping space plausibly sits at **R250k–R600k** **[uncertain: no SA-built toy hauler price found; this is a gap in the market]**.
- **Search terms:** "toy hauler south africa", "toy hauler for sale in south africa", "motorbike trailer for sale south africa", "quad bike trailer for sale south africa", "jet ski trailer for sale south africa". "What is the best toy hauler to buy" and "toy hauler brands to avoid" also appear in ZA suggestions.

### 1.3 The horse owner (competitive rider, livery/stud, Western Cape show circuit: Kraaifontein, Wellington, Kyalami-bound)
- **Wants:** a safe, quiet ride for horses worth far more than the trailer. Good ventilation. Low ramp. Anti-slip, rubber-matted floors. Tack room. Often living quarters for multi-day shows. Increasingly gooseneck living-quarters rigs (US style).
- **Fears:** floor rot and corrosion (urine). Sway. Brake failure. A horse injured by sharp edges. Imports that cannot be serviced locally. Under-rated axles.
- **Trusts:** horse-world references and riders. Recognised European brands (Böckmann, Ifor Williams, Cheval Liberté are what SA dealers stock). Warranty: Bloomer (USA) sells on a "10-year structural warranty" (https://www.bloomertrailers.com).
- **Budget:** new two-horse boxes **R76 900–R480 000**, most R130k–R300k. Living-quarters units from **R260 000**. 3+ horse trucks up to R2.5 m (https://trailersmart.co.za/shop/). Venter two-berth box R204 772 incl VAT ex factory (undated: http://www.ventertrailers.co.za/new-design-horse-box-ex-factory-horse-boxes-trailers-for-sale-2563.aspx).
- **Search terms:** "horse trailer for sale south africa", "horse trailer price", "gooseneck horse trailer with living quarters".

### 1.4 The mobile food / coffee / events operator (start-up entrepreneur through to corporate brand activation)
- **Wants:** a trailer that earns money from day one. A layout matched to the menu. Gas and electrical compliance (Certificate of Compliance for gas/electrics; municipal health certificate of acceptability). Stand-out branding that photographs well. Serving-hatch height. Quick event set-up. Finance.
- **Fears:** failing health or fire inspection. Cheap builds (rusting, poor insulation, overweight). Delays before a season or event. Hidden costs (equipment, licensing). Resale.
- **Trusts:** a portfolio of real operators and brands (Western Cape Trailers names Adidas, Columbia and Hillsong as clients, https://www.westerncapetrailers.co.za). Compliance knowledge. Finance options. Visible prices.
- **Budget:** entry food trailers **R40k–R100k** (Trailer Mania 2.4–4 m R39 980–R56 980, Rakutu from R68 000, Pristine Motors custom kitchen R99 950). Fully equipped/branded units R150k–R500k+ (a Johannesburg coffee trailer business at R195k, Garden Route food trailer business R525k). Sources in section 5.
- **Search terms:** "food trailers for sale cape town", "food trailer manufacturers cape town", "coffee trailer for sale cape town", "mobile kitchen trailer prices in south africa", "how to register a food trailer in south africa".

### 1.5 The farmer / contractor / specialist business (agri, construction, film, solar, medical, municipal)
- **Wants:** a tool on wheels that works hard: livestock, bowsers, generator/solar trailers, mobile workshops, clinic trailers, film-equipment trailers. Goosenecks for heavy, stable loads behind a Land Cruiser 79 or an imported US pickup.
- **Fears:** downtime. Overloading fines. Weak axles and couplings. Paperwork for fleet registration (MIB, tender compliance, B-BBEE, VAT invoices).
- **Trusts:** engineering credentials. Load ratings on the plate. References from other firms. Speed of quotes. Repair and service capacity. The City of Cape Town issued an RFI for "design, manufacture, supply of purpose-built trailers" in 2026 (https://documents.easytenders.co.za/public/tenders/2026/05/223759/rfi164_2025_26-design-manufacture-supply-purpose-built-trailers.pdf).
- **Budget:** wide. Galvanised flat-decks/car trailers R40k–R70k new (Fleetco 4 m tilt double-axle R43 730, https://www.gumtree.co.za/vehicles/westonaria/fleetco-car-trailer-4m-tilt-double-axel-with-single-brake-1-5-ton-ls2430m516k6xyy140p). Heavy goosenecks R145k (used) to R2.2 m (new lowbed) (https://www.junkmail.co.za/trailers/goose-neck).
- **Search terms:** "trailer manufacturers cape town", "trailer builders western cape", "flatbed trailer for sale south africa", "livestock trailers for sale south africa", "custom made trailers south africa".

### 1.6 The gooseneck / fifth-wheel early adopter (US-pickup or Land Cruiser owner, big-caravan or horse-LQ buyer)
- **Wants:** a large, stable living or hauling trailer with US-style gooseneck/5th-wheel towing. Often inspired by American RV/horse culture. Usually tows with a RAM, F-150 or Silverado (converted/imported) or a 79-series.
- **Fears:** legality and registration of an unusual combination. Tow-vehicle conversion (load-bed hitch, GCM). Length limits (18.5 m combination for semi-trailers). Finding someone who has done it before. ZA 4x4 forum discussion of 5th-wheel caravans flags towing rules as the main constraint (https://www.4x4community.co.za/forum/showthread.php/343727-5th-wheel-caravans).
- **Trusts:** proof the builder has registered goosenecks before. Engineering drawings. Hitch installation offered in-house.
- **Budget:** no SA retail benchmark found **[uncertain]**. Imported US gooseneck horse LQ trailers sell at US$55k–80k+ before import (Featherlite dealer listings: https://www.featherlitetrailers.com). Local one-off builds likely R400k–R1.5 m.
- **Search terms:** small ZA volume. "gooseneck trailer south africa" and "gooseneck trailer for sale south africa" exist; "fifth wheel caravan south africa" and "gooseneck trailer cape town" return **no** ZA suggestions, so this is a niche to own, not a traffic driver.

### 2.4 What they all do
1. **Catalogue first, bespoke second.** Even the "custom" builders lead with a model or category grid. "Custom" is one tile among twenty (Western Cape Trailers, Vagabond, Moolman, Intempo).
2. **Hide the price.** Nearly every SA site has "Enquire now" or "price on request". Metalian emails a price list. Mobi Lodge puts its quote builder behind a login. Only Vagabond SA and the dealers publish prices.
3. **Spec-sheet dumps.** Long lists of components (Invader Viator) with no story about why each choice was made.
4. **Generic adventure copy.** "Explore beyond boundaries", "Adventure and memories", "Africa is calling". Testimonials read as made up (Conqueror's three one-liners).
5. **Template sites.** Wix, Weebly, Divi and Duda themes. Copyright years 2020–2021. Thin pages (Intempo). Duplicated copy (Crafted, Atlas). No site shows real design investment.
6. **Weak proof of process.** No one shows the build from bare steel to handover. Workshop, welds and people are almost absent. Metalian's "cut and bent in-house" is the exception, and it is only one sentence.
7. **Compliance as a footnote** (or missing). Only Metalian (NRCS homologation) and Vagabond (SABS) mention it. No one explains licence codes, GVM or registration in plain language, except Vagabond SA's "No EB licence" hook.
8. **Enquiry = a basic form.** Name, phone, email, message, sometimes WhatsApp. No guided brief. No saved configuration (except Vagabond SA and BERG).
9. **Nobody owns toy haulers or goosenecks/fifth-wheels in SA.** No local builder positions on either. Searches for "toy hauler south africa" return US imports. "Fifth wheel caravan south africa" returns no ZA autocomplete suggestions.

### 2.5 How US Trailers can do the opposite
1. **Lead with one-off commissions, not models.** Every build on the site is a client story (the need, the design, the build, the result, the specs), in the style of Cruising Kitchens and Living Vehicle. No catalogue grid.
2. **Show honest price bands** ("Off-road campers from R___; toy haulers typically R___–R___") plus a configurator that gives a live rand range. Vagabond SA proves SA buyers respond to this. Never put it behind a login.
3. **Explain every engineering choice**: why this axle, why galvanised, why this coupler. Make the spec sheet a story (the part-by-part 3D build in CLAUDE.md does exactly this).
4. **Make the workshop the hero**: real welds, real people, a build timeline, named craftsmen. That beats stock adventure imagery.
5. **Make compliance a selling point**: a clear "Legal & licensing" guide covering GVM vs licence code (B vs EB), brakes by reg 151, the 100 km/h bakkie rule, registration, MIB number and NRCS homologation. Offer **build-to-750 kg** options for code-B drivers.
6. **Own the gaps**: dedicated pages for **toy haulers (SA-built)**, **gooseneck & fifth-wheel trailers**, and **modifications/upgrades to existing trailers**. Nobody locally owns these.
7. **A brief, not a form**: a guided enquiry (tow vehicle, licence code, use case, payload, budget band, timeline) that attaches the saved configuration, with WhatsApp handoff.
8. **Trust devices competitors lack**: a written warranty (Bloomer sets the bar at 10-year structural), a build-progress photo diary for every client, handover and registration paperwork done in-house, and dated owner reviews (Living Vehicle style).

## 3. Rules buyers ask about (feeds the FAQ)

Primary source: National Road Traffic Act 93 of 1996 (NRTA) and the National Road Traffic Regulations, 2000 (NRTR), consolidated text on Acts Online (https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/). Note: under s1 of the NRTA a "motor vehicle" includes a trailer, so every NRTR rule written for "motor vehicles" applies to trailers unless excluded. Acts Online is a commercial consolidation of the gazettes, not the Government Printer; quote regulation numbers in the FAQ and add "check with your registering authority".

### 3.1 Registration and licensing (incl. light trailers under 750 kg)
- **Every trailer must be registered and licensed**, whether or not used on a public road, light trailers included. No 750 kg exemption exists for registration. NRTR reg 3 (registration) and reg 18 (licensing). Exemptions in reg 5 cover e.g. non-road vehicles and trailers drawn by a tractor, not light trailers. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_3__motor_vehicle_to_be_re · https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_18__motor_vehicle_to_be_l · https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_5__motor_vehicle_exempt_f
- **Western Cape fees from 1 July 2026** (Provincial Gazette Extraordinary 9210, 20 March 2026): registration R310. Annual licence for a trailer is based on **tare**: 0–250 kg R174 · 251–500 R204 · 501–750 R210 · 751–1 000 R336 · 1 001–1 250 R450 · 1 251–1 500 R612 · 1 501–1 750 R684 · 1 751–2 000 R774 · 2 001–2 250 R1 050 · 2 251–2 500 R1 224 · 2 501–2 750 R1 386 · 2 751–3 000 R1 398 · 3 001–3 250 R1 704 · 3 251–3 500 R1 920. A **caravan** is a flat R354; a farm-only trailer R138. https://www.bergmun.org.za/sites/default/files/9210-WC-Registration-Licence-Fees-2026.pdf
  - FAQ angle: a heavy off-road camper registered as a **caravan** costs R354 a year vs R612–R1 400 as a "trailer". How a build is classified on NaTIS matters. **[uncertain: what qualifies as "caravan" is decided at registration; confirm with the MIB inspector]**

### 3.2 Roadworthy certificate (RWC)
- No annual RWC for private light trailers. Annual RWC is only for goods vehicles >3 500 kg GVM, buses, vehicles carrying people for reward, etc. (NRTR reg 142). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_142__certain_classes_of_m
- An RWC **is** needed before a licence disc is issued when: a used trailer **changes owner**; it was built up from parts by a builder who is not registered; it was **modified or reconstructed**; it was built by an **unregistered** manufacturer/builder; or the MIB conditions say so (NRTR reg 138(1)). Exemption: change of owner within 60 days of first licensing (reg 138(3)(c)). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_138__certification_of_roa
  - FAQ angle: a new trailer from a **registered builder** goes onto NaTIS with the builder's paperwork, so the buyer does not need a roadworthy. A modification job on a customer's existing trailer may trigger a fresh RWC. **[uncertain: confirm with US Trailers' MIB conditions]**

### 3.3 Building trailers legally: MIB registration, NRCS homologation, letters of authority
- **Anyone who manufactures, builds, modifies or imports vehicles (trailers included) as a business must register as an MIB** (manufacturer, builder, importer), NRTR reg 38. The inspectorate checks compliance and SAPS vets the applicant. You get a certificate on form MCR (reg 40). Conditions: notify every model on form **NVM**, hold a **model number for each model**, and possibly an RWC/SAPS clearance per vehicle (reg 41). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_38__certain_manufacturers · https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_40__manner_of_registratio · https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_41__conditions_for_regist
- A builder **not** required to register (e.g. a once-off private build) needs an **NRCS letter of authority (form ALA) for every vehicle**, plus SAPS clearance and an RWC before registration. **Exception: trailers ≤750 kg GVM need no letter of authority** (NRTR reg 43). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_43__manufacturers__builde · https://www.gov.za/services/register-motor-vehicle/letter-authority-respect-motor-vehicle
- **NRCS homologation**: each model and variant must be approved by the NRCS before it can be registered on NaTIS. The NRCS issues a compliance certificate and a NaTIS model number (NRCS presentation, 2022). https://pmg.org.za/files/220223-_NRCS_Panel_vans.pdf. Light trailers and caravans (category **O1**: single axle ≤0.75 t; **O2**: ≤3.5 t) fall under the *Compulsory Specification for Motor Vehicles of Category O1/O2 (Caravans and Light Trailers)*, as amended by GG 32916 of 5 Feb 2010. It excludes prototypes and agricultural trailers unless they are later sold for road use. https://www.tradebarriers.org/ntm/measures/view/3136. Heavy trailers (O3/O4, >3.5 t) have a separate spec. **[uncertain: the spec number is cited as VC 8026 in one listing (https://foresightpublications.co.za/SANS4.html) and VC 8028 in a 2011 gazette. Confirm with the NRCS (012 482 8700).]**
- Trust signal used by competitors: Metalian states "All trailers are homologated with the NRCS" (https://www.metalian.co.za/). Vagabond says "all trailers manufactured are SABS approved" (https://vagabondtrailers.co.za/). US Trailers should state its MIB number and homologation status plainly.
- Search demand exists for this: "how to register a home built trailer in south africa", "...self built trailer", "...newly built trailer" (Google autocomplete, ZA).

### 3.4 Brakes by GVM (NRTR reg 151)
Brake needs depend on the **trailer's GVM compared with the towing vehicle's tare**:

| Trailer GVM | vs tow-vehicle tare | Minimum brakes |
|---|---|---|
| ≤750 kg | ≤ half of tare | parking brake or other device to hold it stationary |
| ≤750 kg | > half but ≤ tare | parking brake + overrun **or** service brake |
| ≤750 kg | > tare | parking brake + service brake |
| 750–3 500 kg | ≤ tare | parking brake + overrun **or** service brake |
| 750–3 500 kg | > tare | parking brake + **service brake** (operable by driver while moving, e.g. electric/electronic) |
| >3 500 kg | any | parking brake + service brake |

Where more than one trailer is towed, the GVMs are added together. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_151__brakes_on_trailers
- Secondary sources simplify this to "unbraked ≤ half tow-vehicle tare up to 750 kg; braked ≤ tow-vehicle tare to max 3 500 kg" (Burquip, https://burquip.co.za/?p=4129). That is **not** what reg 151 says: a heavier trailer is allowed if it has a service brake. The tow vehicle's manufacturer towing rating and GCM still apply (reg 239 for goods vehicles/bakkies: GCM must not be exceeded, https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_239__gross_vehicle_mass__).

### 3.5 Driver's licence codes (NRTR reg 99(4))
- **Code B**: a vehicle with tare ≤3 500 kg (or a bakkie/goods vehicle with GVM ≤3 500 kg), **with a trailer of GVM ≤750 kg**.
- **Code EB**: the same vehicle with a **trailer of GVM >750 kg**, or an articulated vehicle with truck-tractor GCM ≤3 500 kg. The regulation text sets **no 3 500 kg GCM cap** on the "combination" limb, even though many blogs say EB = "max 3 500 kg". **[uncertain: widely misreported; e.g. https://www.tourismtattler.com/a-quick-guide-to-driving-licence-codes-in-south-africa/ claims a GCM cap. Confirm with a DLTC/RTMC before publishing.]**
- **C1**: tare/GVM 3 500–16 000 kg with a trailer ≤750 kg. **EC1**: C1 vehicle with a trailer >750 kg.
- Practical FAQ: almost every off-road camper (GVM typically 1 200–2 000 kg), toy hauler, horse trailer, gooseneck or food trailer >750 kg GVM needs **EB**. Small bike/luggage trailers ≤750 kg GVM are fine on code B. Build-to-750 kg is a real design brief for buyers without EB.
- Source: https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_99__categories_of_learner

### 3.6 Weight limits
- Light trailer limits come mainly from reg 151 (above), the trailer's own GVM/axle ratings, and the tow vehicle's rated towing capacity and GCM (reg 239). Tyres and axles must carry the load.
- A trailer must carry a metal **information plate on its left side** showing tare (T), GVM, axle mass(es) etc. Reg 245 applies this to goods vehicles/trailers. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_245__information_plates_o
- Max axle loads (heavy vehicles): 8 000 kg per two-wheel non-steering axle, 9 000 kg per four-wheel axle (reg 240). Mostly irrelevant below 3.5 t. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_240__massload_carrying_ca

### 3.7 Dimensions
- **Width:** max **2.5 m** for any vehicle other than a bus or a goods vehicle ≥12 000 kg GVM (which get 2.6 m) (reg 223). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_223__overall_width_of_veh
- **Height:** max **4.3 m** including load (reg 224). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_224__overall_height_of_ve
- **Length:** a single-axle (or single axle-unit) trailer ≤12 000 kg GVM: max **8 m excluding drawbar**. Any other vehicle: 12.5 m incl. drawbar. Combination with a semi-trailer (fifth-wheel/gooseneck): **18.5 m**. Any other combination: **22 m** (reg 221). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_221__overall_length_of_ve
- **Drawbar:** max **2 m** on a trailer with more than one axle (reg 222(2)(b)). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_222__restriction_on_combi
- **Rear overhang:** on a single-axle trailer (or tandem axles <1.2 m apart), rear overhang must not exceed 50% of body length (reg 226). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_226__overhang_of_vehicle
- **Goosenecks/fifth-wheels:** semi-trailer front overhang max 1.8 m (reg 226(1)(a)); combination max 18.5 m.

### 3.8 Lights, reflectors and markings
- Rear lamps: red, one each side within 400 mm of the outer edge, 350 mm–1.5 m high (up to 2.1 m if not practical) (reg 168).
- Stop lamps: one each side, 300 mm–2.1 m high, brighter than the rear lamps, triggered by the tow vehicle's brake (reg 169).
- Number-plate lamp: white, plate legible at 20 m (reg 170).
- Indicators: flasher type, within 500 mm of the outer edge, visible at 30 m in daylight, amber/yellow/red to the rear (reg 194).
- Retro-reflectors: **two white on the front of every trailer** (reg 186). Two red at the rear (reg 187). Yellow side reflectors if the combination is >7 m (reg 188), not needed where contour marking is fitted.
- **Contour/strip marking:** every trailer or caravan must have side and rear retro-reflective contour markings to SABS ECE R104 since 1 July 2006 (reg 192A(2)(d)). Commonly missed on custom builds. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_192a__side_and_rear_retro
- Optional side-marker lamps (reg 171).
- Pages: https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_168__rear_lamps · …r225_169__stop_lamps · …r225_170__number_plate_lamps · …r225_194__direction_indicators · …r225_186__white_retro-relector · …r225_187__red_retro-reflectors · …r225_188__yellow_retro-reflect

### 3.9 Towing speed limits
- General limits: 60 km/h urban, 100 km/h rural, 120 km/h freeway (reg 292). No separate lower limit for a **passenger car/SUV** towing a light trailer. https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_292__general_speed_limits
- **Bakkies (goods vehicles) are different.** If a goods vehicle tows one or two trailers and the **sum of the GVMs is over 3 500 kg but not over 9 000 kg**, the limit is **100 km/h everywhere**, and the combination must show a 100 km/h sign at the rear (reg 293(1)(b)(iv)(bb), inserted by Notice 1408 of 2016). Example: a Hilux (GVM ≈3 t) plus a 1 500 kg GVM off-road trailer comes to 4.5 t, so the limit is 100 km/h. Above 9 000 kg it is 80 km/h (reg 293(1)(a)). https://www.acts.co.za/national-road/r225_293__speed_limit_for_part · https://businesstech.co.za/news/trending/143532/transport-department-signs-off-on-new-speed-limit/ **[uncertain: enforcement is patchy and some sources dispute the commencement date. Present it as "the regulation says" with a link.]**
- Trailer tyres must be rated for the speed and load.

## 6. Open questions only the owner can answer

1. **Legal status:** Is US Trailers registered as an MIB with the NRTA/NRCS? Which models or variants are homologated, and do you handle registration paperwork for clients?
2. **Lead products:** Which 2–3 types do you want to lead with (off-road/overland, toy haulers, goosenecks/fifth-wheels, food/coffee, horse, commercial)? Which do you **not** want enquiries for?
3. **Price bands:** Real "from" prices or typical ranges per type, plus your average job value. Can we publish them on the site and in a configurator?
4. **Build time and capacity:** Typical lead time per type, builds per month, and the current waiting list.
5. **Engineering and materials:** Chassis steel and finish (hot-dip galvanised? powder coat?), aluminium/fibreglass capability, axle/suspension and brake brands (e.g. Burquip, Al-Ko, Knott), independent suspension, in-house CNC/laser or bending.
6. **Gooseneck/fifth-wheel and toy-hauler track record:** Have you built and registered these in SA? Which tow vehicles? Do you fit gooseneck/5th-wheel hitches to bakkies or pickups?
7. **Modifications:** What modification/upgrade work do you take on for existing trailers (suspension, canopies, conversions, repairs)? Is there a minimum job size?
8. **Warranty and after-sales:** Structural/workmanship warranty terms, service offering, and support away from Cape Town or cross-border.
9. **Proof:** Past builds with photos/video, client names or testimonials we can use, notable clients, the founder and team story with qualifications (coded welders, engineers), and the workshop address (and whether visits are welcome).
10. **Commercial terms and channels:** Deposit/payment schedule, finance partners, delivery across SA and neighbouring countries, hire/demo units, plus contact details: phone, WhatsApp, email, socials, domain (ustrailers.co.za?).
